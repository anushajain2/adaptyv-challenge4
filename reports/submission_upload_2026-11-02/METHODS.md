# Methods for the TfR1 Track 3 submission

Anusha Jain used a control-calibrated computational workflow to generate human TfR1 apical-domain miniproteins and explore histidine-mediated weakening at acidic pH. The ranked CSV contains bare designed sequences. All candidates are experimental hypotheses: human binding, acidic release, mouse binding and expression have not been measured. Selection combines structural consistency, a proposed pH mechanism and lineage diversity rather than treating a single prediction score as affinity.

## Target and design provenance

Retained parent sequences were generated de novo with BoltzGen revision `247b9bbd8b68a60aba854c2968d6a0cddd21ad6d`, using Adaptyv-derived Modal wrappers. The productive configuration used the bound 6WRW receptor conformation, native human TfR1 residues 190–383, and native hotspot positions 208, 210 and 211. Mapping between native and cropped residue numbering is explicit. Full-dimer and observed-glycan context was restored for steric auditing; observed glycans do not represent all possible glycan conformations. Native position 142 is outside this crop and was handled in full-target preparation.

Known binder complexes were used to calibrate prediction and filtering, not as submitted binder sequences or binder templates. Histidine substitutions and local ProteinMPNN redesigns were made in our own newly generated parent sequences. ProteinMPNN revision `8907e6671bfbfc92303b5f79c4b5e6ce47cdef57` used the vanilla `v_48_020` model, preserving the designed histidine and predefined anchor proxies. ProteinMPNN likelihood was interpreted as sequence compatibility, not pH sensitivity or binding strength.

The campaign retained unsuccessful branches in its records. The earliest 75-design pilot lacked positive hotspot features and cannot support a claim of correct conditioning. Later input generation separated binding and exclusion parser entries and verified actual features. Corrected conserved-patch and nanobody arms did not produce retained parents. Apical miniproteins supplied the candidates discussed here.

## Prediction and structural selection

Refolding inputs supplied receptor coordinates while excluding the entire binder, and the suffix where present, from templates. The single-sequence configuration was chosen from this campaign's controls; this does not imply that MSAs generally reduce prediction quality. Positive controls included crystallographic TfR1 binders, with 2DS25.5 used in later panels. Scrambled sequences and an unrelated VHH were presumed negatives, not experimentally verified nonbinders. Chai-1 did not supply independent interface confirmation.

Model-selection rules were fixed within each round. Later construct comparisons selected the lowest mean receptor-to-binder-core cross-PAE among samples passing a receptor-integrity check. Core scoring avoids dilution by the flexible suffix. Geometry checks assessed fold retention, interface placement after receptor fitting, design-reference contact retention, intended hotspot contacts and full-context clashes. For a novel design, reference-contact retention measures computational self-consistency, not recovery of a known experimental interface. Failed selected samples were not replaced by more favorable alternatives.

The latest 140-prediction comparison used seven sequences, bare/tagged conditions, fresh seeds 3509/3607 and five samples per seed. All 12 selected raw control checks passed. The A41H/A34K/A38R reserve passed the core screen in 9/10 bare and 10/10 tagged predictions; all four selected reserve models passed strict preparation checks. Fresh bare A41H passed 9/10 and its original parent passed 10/10. Pass counts are reported per configuration, not pooled into a biological hit rate. Full results and exclusions are in the [reserve comparison](https://github.com/anushajain2/adaptyv-challenge4/blob/4a92a78c91e66cc8fa4966e3eac93e6c3695cfa8/reports/reserve_tag/RESULTS.md).

## Proposed pH mechanism and limitations

Free and bound PROPKA calculations compare an extracted binder and its complex in the same prepared conformation. The protocol applies Amber14/GBn2 preparation uniformly, with both backbones restrained and side chains mobile. It checks movement, severe internal overlaps and protonation failures. Preparation does not rescue a raw structural failure. The reported histidine-only Kd6/Kd7.4 quantity is a conditional proton-linkage proxy, not measured affinity or a full binding free-energy calculation.

The frozen direction heuristics required a pKa decrease of at least 0.5 units, a free histidine pKa in 6–7, a ratio proxy of at least 2, and adequate distance from artificial receptor termini. The latest bare A41H poses gave shifts of −0.980/−0.722; the reserve gave −1.121/−0.961, consistent in direction with its earlier −1.042/−1.046. However, their latest bare free pKas exceed 7. Both conditions also contain negative-control preparation failures, so no fully qualified pH switch is claimed. PROPKA decomposition primarily attributes the reserve's shift to burial/desolvation within the model, rather than demonstrated direct histidine–cation repulsion. Unbound conformational sampling and experimental pH validation remain outstanding.

The 64-residue public platform-default linker/GFP11/Twin-Strep suffix was modeled as an explicit assumption; the exact challenge construct was not confirmed. Only binder-core His41 contributes to the cited ratio, while complete suffix chemistry remains in tagged pKa calculations. Flexible suffix contacts were inconsistent and low confidence. They establish neither assay interference nor tag independence. Submission sequences omit this suffix, and the designed binding interface is in the binder core.

## Diversity and sequence review

Lineages are counted by their generated parent; point mutants and their parent do not count as independent scaffolds. Ranking is an editorial experimental priority, not a calibrated probability of binding. Candidate-specific caveats accompany the ranking, including mixed geometry, a modeled C31–C42 disulfide in diversity_short_055, and sequence motifs whose experimental effects are unknown. Mouse predictions were inconclusive because the matched human-model control failed; no cross-reactivity claim is made.

Earlier parent reviews used RCSB sequence searches, Foldseek PDB100/AlphaFold-Swiss-Prot comparisons, TM-align and local BLASTp against UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot canonical release 2026_03. No reviewed parent met the predefined close-sequence threshold, but short_015 had a close backbone match to AF1782/2OO2. Database coverage, short-sequence sensitivity and shared folds limit novelty conclusions. A parent's search result is not a new search of a later mutant. See the [original review](https://github.com/anushajain2/adaptyv-challenge4/blob/4a92a78c91e66cc8fa4966e3eac93e6c3695cfa8/reports/novelty_review/RESULTS.md) and [diversity review](https://github.com/anushajain2/adaptyv-challenge4/blob/4a92a78c91e66cc8fa4966e3eac93e6c3695cfa8/reports/diversity_novelty/RESULTS.md).
